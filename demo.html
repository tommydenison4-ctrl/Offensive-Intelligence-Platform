Know the source

Three inputs. One useful game record.

ESPN · VERIFIED IN TEST FEED
  • Score, game status, quarter and clock
  • Drive possession and play identifiers
  • Down, distance and field position
  • Play description, yardage and event type
  • Scoring and penalty information in events/text
Names can appear in descriptions. Dedicated passer, receiver and rusher fields were not populated in our test. Feed timing and field completeness vary.
OUR COORDINATOR’S CALL SHEET
  • Exact call and call-sheet section
  • Offense: formation, motion, family
  • Run concept and protection, where defined
  • Defense: front, coverage and pressure
  • Expected rush count, where defined
Mappings need coordinator review. Called and executed remain separate when there is a check or change.
MANUAL OPPONENT TAGS
  • Against our offense: front and coverage
  • Pressure, actual rush count and box count
  • Against our defense: formation and motion
  • Opponent play family and run concept
  • Optional hash, gap and target zone
Personnel and detailed technique need observation. ESPN does not supply a PFF-style coaching breakdown.
What we need from the coordinators

One call sheet. One terminology review.

Provide a PDF, spreadsheet or clear photo, plus a key for shorthand. Review the call-to-tag mapping once. Each call can then become a tile that fills our side of the record.

Presentation prototype. Call-sheet importing, automatic matching, recovery controls and these expanded reports are proposed workflows. The separate game-day test app currently supports ESPN loading, manual tagging and device-local saving. Default example rules: downs 1–3, no no-plays, explosives 15+ run / 20+ pass, negatives ≤0.
